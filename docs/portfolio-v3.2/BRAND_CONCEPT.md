# JF Signal — brand concept

## Chosen direction

`Signal Spine` is a compact JF monogram drawn as one software route. The path enters from the lower left, resolves the curve of the J, rises into a shared spine, and exits through two precise F branches. A single blue node marks the controlled delivery point.

The mark carries the same visual grammar as the portfolio: routes, nodes, system boundaries and deliberate delivery. Its identity comes from geometry rather than a container, glow or gradient.

## Brand principles

- The white route is the stable system; blue is reserved for a live signal.
- The silhouette remains legible without motion, gradient or wordmark.
- Square terminals echo the technical line work already used by the architecture maps.
- The route has few control points and survives 16 px rendering.
- Motion is event-driven: one short intro and a hover replay, never a permanent loop.

## Lockups

- Full: symbol, Jonathan Febraio, Software Engineer.
- Compact: symbol and name.
- Mark only: favicon, narrow mobile header and social signature.
- Monochrome: one-color printing and environments without the signal accent.

## Motion language

The route draws in 720 ms, the middle branch follows, and a blue pulse travels once to the delivery node. Hover repeats only the signal pass and briefly releases three endpoint particles. `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` renders the completed mark immediately.

## Five-question review

1. Recognizable without the name: yes, at 16–64 px in the prototype.
2. Represents systems and flow: yes, one input route, shared spine and two outputs.
3. Fits the portfolio: yes, it reuses the established grid, route and node language.
4. Reads as an international professional mark: yes, with restrained color and no decorative container.
5. Remains strong without animation or gradient: yes; the monochrome silhouette is complete.
